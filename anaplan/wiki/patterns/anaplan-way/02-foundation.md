# Phase 2 — Foundation

> Everything that must be done **before sprints begin**. Sets the project up for execution.

The phase is built around **Project Planning** — typically **4–5 days** to draft the project timeline. It contains six activities:

1. [L1 model-builder training](#l1-model-builder-training)
2. [Project kick-off meeting](#project-kick-off-meeting)
3. [Manifesto](#manifesto)
4. [Establish the scrum team](#establish-the-scrum-team)
5. [Deployment plan](#deployment-plan)
6. [CoE setup](#establish-a-coe)

---

## L1 model-builder training

Customer model builders complete L1 training — they need it to participate meaningfully in the build.

## Project kick-off meeting

Introduces the objectives and the Anaplan Way methodology to the wider organization.

**Suggested agenda**:

- **Executive sponsor introduction** — explains *why* they chose Anaplan.
- **Team introductions and time expectations** — the **full team** should attend.
- **Assess and state the current status**.
- **Methodology overview** (agile/scrum).
- **Customer end-to-end process** walkthrough.

## Manifesto

A **clear, concise statement of what the team intends to build**, focused on the overall project goal. Written by the **customer**, with the consultant helping.

### Why a manifesto matters

- **Steers the project back on track** — anchors everyone to the MVP. *(Note: don't say "MVP" out loud to the client — it can undermine confidence in Anaplan. Frame it as "Release 1" instead.)*
- **Creates a sense of purpose** — leaves people inspired.
- Becomes a **communication tool with executives**.

### Manifesto rules

- Focus on **foundations first** — keep it simple.
- Define **what success means** and **how to measure it**.
- **All stakeholders involved** in writing it.

## Establish the scrum team

(See [[00-fundamentals#roles-the-scrum-team|Fundamentals § Roles]] for the role definitions.)

- **Project Sponsor** — customer side; owns ROI, vision, prioritization.
- **Scrum Master** — facilitator without management authority; ideally customer side.
- **Scrum team** — 5–10 cross-functional members, full-time, self-organizing, includes customer staff.

## Deployment plan

> The project team must convince the rest of the business of the value of the Anaplan model — otherwise the model and process changes won't be adopted.

Deployment-plan checklist:

- **Over-communicate** that a process change and a new tool are coming.
- **Estimate the user population** to be trained — and what resources you need to reach them.
- **Create a training plan** for end users. Include:
  - *Why* the change matters to the user
  - *What* the change includes
  - *How* their work will change
  - *Where* they can get help
- **Use documentation to create job aids** on process flows and changes.
- **Identify 2–3 success measures** — how will you know users are adopting the model and process?

## Establish a CoE

> All projects should be set up for a future **Center of Excellence** — not just large projects.

The CoE is a team that provides leadership, best practices, research, support, and training for Anaplan inside the organization. Preparing for one means:

- **Set up a Data Hub as part of the model design.** Makes adding future use cases much cheaper.
- **Document business processes** — when a new use case is considered, you can place it in context.
- **Document the model logic and business rules**:
  - Technical ecosystem topology
  - Model blueprint
  - Module blueprints
  - Model flow / model map / schema

## Tollgate → Design

Before moving on:

- Is the scrum team formed and full-time committed?
- Manifesto written and signed off by all stakeholders?
- Deployment plan drafted?
- L1 training complete for customer model builders?
- Data conversation initiated with IT?
