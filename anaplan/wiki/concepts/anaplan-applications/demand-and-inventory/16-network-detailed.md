# Exercise 16 — Configuring a Distribution Network

**Series:** Anaplan Demand & Supply Chain Reference App
**Chapter:** 16 of 17
**Lessons:** 16 (single lesson)

---

## Overview

Exercise 16 extends the inventory plan from a collection of independent, self-sourcing distribution centres into a **multi-tiered distribution network**, where upstream DCs replenish downstream ones. The worked example uses three DCs: a **Central DC** that sources inventory from production and replenishes a **North DC** and a **South DC**.

Without this configuration, each DC's replenishment requirement is open-ended — the model knows that the North and South DCs need more inventory, but has no information about where that inventory will come from. Exercise 16 closes that loop by defining the supply relationships between DCs, the lanes through which goods flow, and the lead times that govern the timing of those flows.

Once configured, the Central DC sees two distinct sources of demand: its own direct customer demand, and the replenishment demand generated by the North and South. Its own replenishment requirement then becomes the **signal to production** — the upstream trigger that tells the network how much new inventory needs to enter the system as a whole.

The exercise also briefly introduces **ad hoc network transfers** as a related but distinct concept, and closes with a note on how to extend the configuration to networks with more than two tiers.

## The Problem: What Was Missing Before This Exercise

At the end of Exercise 15, the inventory plan had the following structure:

1. Customer demand was disaggregated to DC level — each DC knew how much it needed to serve its own customers directly.
2. That direct customer demand triggered a **replenishment requirement** at each DC — the model calculated how much additional inventory each DC needed to bring in.
3. But the model had not yet been told **where that replenishment would come from**. The requirement was open-ended: the North and South DCs needed inventory, but the plan had no mechanism to connect that need to the Central DC as the source.

Exercise 16 fills this gap. The goal is to reach a state where:

- The North and South DCs' replenishment requirements are recognised as **demand on the Central DC**, not just as open-ended sourcing needs.
- The Central DC's own replenishment requirement — now reflecting both customer demand and the need to supply North and South — becomes the production signal for the entire network.

## Step 1 — Create Empty Transfer Lane Placeholders (Page 904)

### Where to work

Navigate to **page 904 — Lists: Create Empty Placeholders** within the Inventory Planning application.

### What this step does

The application follows a consistent pattern for planning inputs: rather than having users create new list items directly, it **pre-creates empty placeholder items** that users then populate with data. This approach ensures that planners are always working within a controlled list structure rather than adding ad hoc entries.

Page 904 is the **Location Related Lists** management page. It displays a table of all location-related lists in the application, each with two configurable parameters:

- **Number to Create** — the default number of placeholder items to generate per location.
- **Threshold to Create %** — when automatic list updates are required, this threshold determines at what point the list is flagged for replenishment. Specifically, if the number of items currently in use exceeds this percentage of the total created, the list is flagged to be updated with additional placeholders.

The lists managed on this page include factory calendar events, holiday calendars, constraint events, inventory expiration events, work centres, and — most relevant to this exercise — **TL031 - Transfer Lanes #**. In the reference app, TL031 is configured with a default of **5 placeholders per location** and a threshold of **20%**.

The lower section of the page shows a per-location breakdown: for each DC (Central, Northern, Southern), it displays the number of records created, how many are currently in use, and a **Create?** checkbox to include that location in the next run.

### Action

Click **Create Location Related Lists** and wait for the process to complete. This generates 5 empty Transfer Lane placeholder items per DC into list TL031, ready to be populated on page 910. Once done, the placeholders are available and the next configuration step can begin.

## Step 2 — Define Transfer Lanes and Routine Disaggregation (Page 910)

### Where to work

Navigate to **page 910 — Manage Transfer Lanes**.

### Understanding the lane structure

Each transfer lane in the application is structured as a **child of the destination DC** — the DC that receives the inventory. When configuring a lane, the user selects the **"From DC"** (the source) from a picker. This means:

- The North DC lane: child of North DC → From DC = Central DC
- The South DC lane: child of South DC → From DC = Central DC

This structure reflects the direction of inventory flow: inventory moves *from* the Central DC *into* the North and South.

### Configuring the lanes

For each lane, three inputs are required:

**1. From DC (source)**
Select the DC that will supply inventory through this lane. In the example, both the North and South lanes have Central as the source.

**2. Routine disaggregation percentage**
This defines what proportion of the destination DC's total replenishment requirement is met through this specific lane. In the example, 100% of the North DC's replenishment comes from Central, and 100% of the South DC's replenishment comes from Central. In a more complex network, a DC might be supplied by multiple sources, and the disaggregation percentages across all lanes to that DC would need to sum to 100%.

**3. Transit mode**
Select the mode of transport for the lane — in the example, **road**. The transit mode is the key that links the lane to its lead time: different transit modes carry different lead times, allowing the model to distinguish between, say, road and air freight on the same lane if needed.

After completing these inputs, both lanes are defined: Central → North and Central → South, each carrying 100% of the destination DC's replenishment requirement via road.

## Step 3 — Set Lead Times Per Lane (Page 912)

### Where to work

Navigate to **page 912 — Edit Lead Times**.

### What this step does

The transit mode selected in Step 2 governs which lead time applies to a lane. Page 912 is where those lead times are actually entered, per lane and transit mode combination.

**Action:** enter the lead time for each lane. In the example, a conservative planning assumption of **1 period (1 week)** is applied to both the Central → North and Central → South road lanes. This means:

- Inventory shipped out of the Central DC in week N is received at the North or South DC in week N+1.
- The model offsets ship-out and receipt dates by this lead time when calculating inventory flows.

## Step 4 — Validate Inventory Flows (Page 222)

### Where to work

Navigate to **page 222 — Inventory Transfers → Routine Network Replenishment**. Before reviewing the data, ensure the correct **scenario** is selected (Scenario 1 in the example) and that the DC filter is set to show **all DCs**.

### What this page shows

Page 222 displays the volume of inventory flowing through each transfer lane on a weekly basis. For each lane, two time-stamped views are available:

- **Receipt date at destination:** the week in which inventory arrives at the North or South DC.
- **Ship-out date from source:** the same volume, offset back by the lead time, showing the week the Central DC dispatched it.

### Success criterion for this step

If both lanes (Central → North and Central → South) are showing weekly volumes with the correct receipt and ship-out timing, the network configuration is correctly wired up. The flows visible here confirm that the model is routing the North and South DCs' replenishment requirements through the Central DC as defined.

## Step 5 — Review the Impact on the Inventory Plan (Page 240)

### Where to work

Navigate to **page 240 — DC Inventory Planning**.

This is where the full impact of the network configuration becomes visible. The page has a **tier selector** at the top, which controls how much of the demand picture is included in the view. Understanding this selector is essential for interpreting the inventory plan correctly once a network is configured.

---

### Viewing the Central DC

**Tier 1 only**

With the tier selector set to Tier 1, the Central DC's demand shows only its **direct customer demand** — the combination of committed open sales orders and the forecasted demand for customers it serves directly. This is the same picture the Central DC had before Exercise 16.

**Tier 1 and 2**

Switching to Tier 1 and 2 adds a second demand stream: the **simulated network replenishment demand**. This is the volume of inventory the Central DC must supply to the North and South DCs to meet their replenishment requirements. It appears as an additional demand layer on the chart, labelled as simulated network replenishment.

The Central DC's own replenishment calculation — the simulated receipts it needs from production — now reflects this combined demand picture. It is no longer just serving its own customers; it is also absorbing the replenishment burden of the North and South. This combined replenishment requirement is the **production signal for the entire network**.

---

### Viewing the North and South DCs

**Tier 1 only**

With the tier selector at Tier 1, the North or South DC shows its replenishment requirement as an **open-ended simulated requirement** — inventory that the model knows is needed but has not yet attributed to a specific source. This is the same state the model was in before Exercise 16 was configured.

**Tier 1 and 2**

Switching to Tier 1 and 2 changes the picture: the replenishment requirement is now recognised as inventory to be **received through the network** — specifically, from the Central DC via the configured transfer lane. The simulated requirement line is replaced by (or labelled as) a "planned to receive from network replenishment" flow, confirming that the sourcing relationship is fully wired.

---

### The tier selector in practice

The lesson makes an important operational point: once network configuration is in place, planners should **always use the highest tier available** when reviewing the inventory plan. Lower tiers give a partial picture — they either exclude downstream replenishment demand (for the Central DC) or show sourcing as open-ended (for the North and South). The full picture, which is what the planner needs to make decisions, is only visible at the highest tier.

---

### Success criterion for the exercise

The exercise is considered complete when:

- The **Central DC** at Tier 1+2 shows the additional simulated network replenishment demand from its role of supplying North and South.
- The **North and South DCs** at Tier 1+2 show their replenishment requirement recognised as "planned to receive from network" rather than open-ended.

## Ad Hoc Network Transfers

The lesson briefly mentions **ad hoc network transfers** as a related but distinct concept. These are occasional, one-off inventory rebalancing moves between DCs — for example, transferring excess stock from the South DC to the North DC to cover a localised shortage. They are distinct from the routine, lane-driven flows configured in this exercise in two ways:

- They are **occasional** rather than driven by a routine cadence.
- They are **not governed by the transfer lane structure** defined on page 910.

Ad hoc transfers are not configured as part of this exercise and are noted only as an extension topic.

---

## Extending to More Than Two Tiers

The two-tier network (Central → North/South) is the example used in the lesson, but the same approach can be extended to deeper, more complex distribution networks.

Additional tiers are configured by **adding versions in the underlying Anaplan model**. Each version represents one replenishment loop around the network. The application uses the **last version in the version list** as the current live plan; additional versions enable deeper tier rollups when the selector is moved to higher tiers.

This is a notable Anaplan modelling choice: rather than adding new structural elements or additional lane attributes to represent extra tiers, the Versions dimension is repurposed as a tier index. This keeps the model architecture consistent while allowing the network depth to be extended incrementally.

## Key concepts

**Multi-tiered distribution network** — a network structure in which upstream DCs replenish downstream ones, rather than all DCs sourcing inventory independently from production.

**Transfer lane** — a configured supply relationship between two DCs, defining the source, the proportion of replenishment demand it carries (routine disaggregation %), and the transit mode. Lanes are children of the destination DC. Stored in list **TL031 - Transfer Lanes #**; placeholder items are pre-created per DC via the Create Location Related Lists process on page 904 before being populated on page 910.

**Routine disaggregation %** — the proportion of a destination DC's total replenishment requirement that is met through a specific transfer lane. If a DC is supplied by a single upstream DC, this is 100%. If it has multiple sources, the percentages across all lanes must sum to 100%.

**Transit mode** — the mode of transport associated with a lane (e.g. road, air). Determines which lead time applies to the lane.

**Lead time (network)** — the offset between the ship-out date at the source DC and the receipt date at the destination DC. Entered per lane and transit mode on page 912.

**Tier selector** — the control on page 240 that determines how much of the demand picture is shown. Tier 1 shows direct customer demand only. Tier 1+2 adds the replenishment demand from downstream DCs. Planners should always use the highest tier for a complete picture.

**Production signal** — the upstream replenishment requirement of the Central (top-tier) DC, which represents the total new inventory that needs to enter the network to satisfy all layers of demand — both direct customer demand and the replenishment needs of downstream DCs.

**Ad hoc network transfers** — occasional, one-off inventory rebalancing moves between DCs, distinct from the routine, lane-driven flows configured in this exercise.

**Versions as tier index** — the Anaplan modelling pattern used to represent additional network tiers. Each version in the underlying model corresponds to one replenishment loop; adding a version adds a tier to the network.
